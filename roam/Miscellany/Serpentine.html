Serpentine

07-12-2025

I’ll try to err on the shorter side.

The AI space has grown and (IMO) saturated quite a lot! I’ve got a bitter aftertaste on the direction of this field after some time at a research firm. There’s many reasons for this, too many for me to compile comprehensively. A few of them are:

  • At the time, I found that “AI’s problem”1 was (and still is) poorly defined. Consequently, basic research in AI loses significant meaning in many contexts.
  • The typical use cases of AI leave me uninspired and admittedly disappointed. You can have it make a new image (arguable)… you can have it write a better email… you can have it plan your vacations… lunch recommendations… These things just don’t register as genuine problems in my mind. Obviously AI can be genuinely useful in the right setting, but the typical use-cases just aren’t it for me…
  • I’d rather help solve problems instead of advertise. This is a jaded opinion about the state of AI/ML research, but I like my lemons sour. I’ve also got a whole lot to learn about building solutions, but I like learning so that’s ok.

I’ve been learning to be more intentional about the problems I want to solve. I had a case of burn out2 after finishing my PhD and convinced myself that I shouldn’t be picky and there are many interesting problems out there3. Just wanted to get out of academics and AI/ML seemed like an open door into the applied sector4. I made the decision in the late summer of 2021, before the popularization of today’s genAI systems. Perhaps this is me just trying to resolve my conscience, however. Regardless, it’s ok to be picky and try to do something cool.

That said, I don’t necessarily disagree with the use of genAI when used responsibly and intentionally. Obviously this can become pretty subjective, but for sure there’s lots of irresponsible use of genAI (just as any technology). I do think that AI has brought lots ot the cybersecurity field (good+bad). Of course, there’s huge questions on how we can secure genAI systems. There’s also lots of application in using genAI systems to solve “classical” problems in cyber. All I’m saying is that it’s a 2-way street here. Selfishly, I also view cybersecurity as an area where, at some point, I can reacquaint myself with some nice math.
